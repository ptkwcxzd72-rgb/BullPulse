What BullPulse measures

Four signals. One score you can see through.

Each stock is ranked against its own market, so a UK score of 80 means the top fifth of UK shares. Every number links back to the evidence behind it.

Trend 40%

Is the price above its 200-day average, with the 50-day above it and the Super Trend signal long?

Momentum 40%

The 12-month return, skipping the latest month: one of the most persistent effects in market research.

Sentiment 20%

Thousands of headlines a night, scored by FinBERT. Company-specific news counts most; market noise counts least.

Growth shown

Growth from company filings, a 30-point fundamentals check, a fair value you can tune, and insider buying from SEC Form 4.

Growth, fundamentals and fair value are displayed and screened on, but not yet weighted into the score. It joins only if the monthly validation shows it helps.

How it works

While the market sleeps, BullPulse takes its pulse.

Every weekday after the New York close, the whole pipeline runs by itself and publishes a fresh ranking by morning.

After the close

Prices arrive

End-of-day prices for every stock, checked for gaps, stale data and unadjusted splits.

Next

News is read

Headlines are gathered, duplicates removed, and each one scored positive, neutral or negative.

Then

Stocks are scored

Trend, momentum and sentiment become percentiles within each market, combined into one score.

By morning

The site updates

New ranks, flags and charts are published for anyone to open, no login needed, with alerts for every stock as RSS feeds.

Inside the screener

Every rank comes with its reasons.

  • Linked price, news-tone, RSI and volume charts for every stock
  • Upside and downside ranges from each stock's own volatility, not price targets
  • Early trend changes and a "growing & turning up" screen
  • A watchlist kept in your own browser, with no account

Built on evidence, not hype

We test the score. In public. Every month.

A monthly backtest checks whether higher-scoring stocks really went on to beat lower-scoring ones, using only data available at the time and including trading costs. The results are published on the screener, good or bad.
