Early trend changes are stocks whose long-term trend turned up recently (a golden cross within 60 days, or a move back above the 200-day average within 40 days), with momentum holding up, news tone improving and RSI below 70. Many early turns fail. These are candidates for closer research, not predictions of fast growth.
Growing & turning up are companies whose latest reported revenue grew 15% or more year on year, with growth holding or speeding up (from SEC filings in the US, annual reports in the UK), and whose long-term price trend is up or has just turned up. Revenue growth is backward-looking and often already priced in. This is a research screen, not a forecast.
Strong fundamentals are companies passing at least 20 of the 30 checks in the Fundamentals Profile (value, growth, track record, financial health and dividends), computed from their filings and today's price. The profile describes the business as reported; it does not predict the share price and is not part of the score.

Insider dealing open-market trades on SEC Form 4 · last 90 days

Directors and officers must report trades in their own company's shares within two business days. Purchases with their own money are the informative ones; insiders sell for many reasons, such as tax or planned sales. Two or more different insiders buying is flagged as cluster buying.

Fundamentals profile

Thirty pass-or-fail checks, six in each area, from the company's filings (SEC filings in the US, the iXBRL annual report in the UK) and today's price. Comparisons are with the other companies in the same market. "Growth" uses reported results, not analyst forecasts. A dash means the data isn't in the filings, and counts as a fail. Not part of the score.

Fair value estimate (DCF) adjust the assumptions

A two-stage discounted cash flow. Cash flow to the whole business (operating cash flow less capital spending and share-based pay, before interest), averaged over three years, grows at the chosen rate for five years, slows to the long-run rate over the next five, and is discounted at the required return; net debt is then subtracted. Bear and bull move growth by 3 points and the return by 1 point. The reverse calculation shows what growth today's price assumes. A rough guide, very sensitive to the inputs, and not a price target.

      Where the numbers come from

      Each night end-of-day bars are synced from Tiingo into the library's file repository with its own indicator-sync tool. A Go job runs trend.Sma, momentum.Rsi, volatility.BollingerBands, volume.Obv and the Golden Cross and Super Trend strategies over every stock, then writes this page's JSON.

      How news is scored

      Articles from GDELT (and Finnhub for US names) are matched to companies, de-duplicated, and scored by FinBERT as positive, neutral or negative. Tone is weighted by relevance and averaged over 30 days; a burst of coverage raises a news flag.

      Running cost: free

      GitHub Actions runs the nightly jobs and GitHub Pages serves this static page, both free for a public repository. Scores are a research filter for further reading. They are not a forecast and not investment advice.

      Ranks are percentiles within each market, so a UK score of 80 and a US score of 80 mean "top fifth of its own market", not equal strength. Backtested and simulated results are hypothetical. Prices for UK stocks are shown in pence.
